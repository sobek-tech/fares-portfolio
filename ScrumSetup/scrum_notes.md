# Scrum

## Scrum vs Waterfall

### Waterfall

Plan -> Build -> Test -> Review -> Deploy

- It starts with planning, which may take several months. Then it moves to the build phase, and if something goes wrong, it goes back to planning, which can be expensive to fix, and so on.
- The problem with Waterfall is that it is not always good because it takes too much time, involves a lot of back-stepping, and could deliver the wrong product.

### Scrum

It consists of Sprints, and every Sprint takes from 1–3 weeks. Every Sprint has planning, building, testing, and reviewing. It starts with enough planning to get you started. It continuously updates, gets feedback quickly, and adapts to changing requirements.

One Sprint: Plan -> Build -> Test -> Review. There may be many Sprints, for example, one Sprint for each new feature.

## 3 Main Roles

### Product Owner

Responsible for new features and represents the business or customer needs. The Product Owner creates user stories.

A user story defines the importance of the feature and follows:

`As a [type of user], I want [a feature], so that [benefit].`

The Product Owner prioritizes the tasks, gives feedback, reviews the task, can accept or reject the work, clarifies requirements, and works with stakeholders and Developers.

### Scrum Master

Protects the team and removes any obstacles that can interrupt or slow down the process.

### Team

Developers, DevOps engineers, testers, etc.

## Scrum Artifacts

### Product Backlog

Where the Product Owner prioritizes the key features that will turn into a product by using user stories.

### Sprint Backlog

- The Sprint Goal.
- The Product Backlog items selected for the current Sprint.
- The Developers’ plan for completing them.

### Charts

- Show the progress of tasks in the Sprint.
- They should show zero points when all the work has been completed.

## Scrum Ceremonies

### Sprint Planning

Where the whole team, including the Product Owner and Scrum Master, meets and discusses the requirements, user stories, and relative size.

### Daily Scrum

Where the team meets every day for 10–15 minutes to discuss what they have done, what they will do, and whether there are any problems.

### Sprint Review

At the end of the Sprint, the team presents its work, and the Product Owner accepts or rejects the work.

### Sprint Retrospective

Focuses on improving the team's process: what went wrong in this Sprint and how the team can improve the process for the next Sprint.

## Flow

```text
Product Backlog
       ↓
Sprint Planning
       ↓
Sprint Backlog
       ↓
Sprint Execution
       ↓
Daily Scrum each day
       ↓
Completed Increment
       ↓
Sprint Review
       ↓
Sprint Retrospective
       ↓
Next Sprint
```

First, the Product Owner creates a Product Backlog that contains the requirements, bright ideas, and features that can go into the product. The Product Owner prioritizes the list and creates the user stories. Then, there is a meeting called Sprint Planning, where everyone meets to discuss the most important user stories, their size, and what can go into the next Sprint.

The output of the Sprint Planning meeting is the Sprint Backlog, which contains the user stories that will be implemented in the current Sprint. Everyone should have a solid understanding of what is in the backlog.

The Sprint Review happens at the end of the Sprint, where the Product Owner accepts or rejects the work and discusses what can be improved. The Sprint Retrospective focuses on how the team can improve the process flow for the next Sprint.
